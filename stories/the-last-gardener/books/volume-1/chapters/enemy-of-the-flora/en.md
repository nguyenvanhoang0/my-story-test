The moment human consciousness staged a brute-force intervention into plant instincts, the entire capillary system went on an immediate strike.

"System Error." Osmotic pressure shattered completely. Water ceased to defy gravity and no longer ascended the stem.

Worse still, the stomata, forced wide open under his command, allowed precious water reserves to transpire uncontrollably into the environment, while the process of CO2 fixation ground to a total halt. A horrific suffocation washed over him.

It was identical to the worst case of "sleep paralysis" a human could endure, but on a cellular level. The plant body began to wither, woody fibers constricting in agonizing spasms. Benny wanted to gape his mouth for a yawn, wanted his chest to heave for a breath of oxygen, wanted to flail his limbs, but he had none of them.  His consciousness shrieked incessantly, thrashing frantically within a rigid, immobile fibrous shell.

Absolute powerlessness consumed his mind. 

"Help... help..." Benny’s distress signal came out distorted, transmitting sheer, unadulterated panic.

Hope’s image flickered, calling out to him, but he could no longer hear her. Panic had entirely swallowed his reason. Her voice grew distorted, fading as if echoing from the bottom of deep water. His optical field went dark. The more his human survival instinct thrashed, the tighter the roots clenched and the more the leaves wilted. At the precise moment his phantom brain reached its breaking point, night fell.

Benny... blacked out.

He lost consciousness entirely, falling into a hollow void. And it was that very forced shutdown that saved his life.

The moment his human soul ceased its brute-force intervention, the primal defense mechanism of the plant immediately took over the body. A faint *click* resonated deep within the biological system. The wide-open stomata instantly snapped shut to prevent further water loss.

The xylem began to flow once more. Root pressure and surface tension automatically drew every drop of moisture from the moss upward into the stem. Water seeped through cell walls with a gentle ease, true to the ancient rhythm of nature. Sunlight piercing through the canopy again softly kissed the chloroplasts.

Who knows how much time passed before Benny slowly regained consciousness. What greeted him was not the stifling grasp of death, but a euphoric, cooling sensation coursing through his entire form. Life had returned. F.E. began to generate smoothly and steadily. It was pure, not much, but enough to make Benny feel as though he’d just received a shot of stimulant straight to his nerves.

This time, he didn't try to force himself to "breathe." Benny let the forest's characteristic damp breeze filter through his leaves, gently swaying his green tendrils, comfortably enjoying survival exactly as a vine should.

He focused on a quick self-check. Mmm, everything was operating normally. The woody core was stable; the leaves remained intact, plump and turgid, showing no signs of wilting.

Where was Hope?

He adjusted his foliage to optimize sun exposure, then searched for her white silhouette. If he called out now, he was pretty sure he’d get chewed out again.

Scanning the surroundings, it took nearly two minutes before he spotted her beneath a small cluster of flowers. In reality, to her, that plant was still a giant tree. Hope circled the small flower, evaluating it a few times before leaving satisfied, approaching a hanging cluster of blossoms not far away.

Curious, Benny tossed a scan command at the plant she had just left.

[Biological scan...] Species Name: Softly Description: The flower never fully opens. Petals are always slightly closed, as if listening. When the wind blows, it doesn't vibrate visibly but only "shifts" so subtly that if you don't look closely, you’d think you imagined it. Discoverer: EMP-F-0200-SPK

*Softly?* So she was out farming contribution points too.

Benny glanced at several other plants in the distance he hadn't noticed earlier: Quiet Tear, Where Light Pauses Before Leaving, Windfinger, Tender.

"So cringy!" Benny shuddered and clicked his tongue. If he were the one naming them, these weeds would have far more practical names, like: Crybaby, Defective Lightbulb, or simply Wimp.

"Awake already?" Hope's voice drifted over.

The little plant cringed. Dammit, he’d accidentally broadcasted his thoughts as a signal.

"A new record! You just broke the death speedrun record for rookies."

*Damn, here it comes.*

"The guy ranked right behind you only lost to you by exactly two minutes."

"Hey... you're a rookie too..." Benny muttered back.

Hope stood with her hands on her hips, evaluating him from tip to root before sticking her nose up in the air.

"Rookie? I dropped that title while this tree was still a seed. Now, I'm a veteran employee with two hundred days of experience."

As expected, those salty remarks were as precise and painful as ever. Benny slightly moved his leaves to cover the tip of his stem, trying to pretend he was still sick. Her narcissistic rambling continued to auto-play like an endless radio broadcast.

Well, actually, it only lasted two minutes after he ignored her. Benny slightly lifted a leaf to peek. The white silhouette was huffily flying toward a flower base a few meters away before flopping down onto the air. Probably the bed in her room. He slightly shook his foliage to test her reaction, but she seemed truly sulky. Turning her back on him, she was sprawling unladylike. Where was her femininity? Where was the poetic demeanor from earlier? Looking at that posture, it was an absolute eyesore!

Benny withdrew his gaze and focused his mind on sensing every rare ray of sunlight shining down. If anyone asked what photosynthesis tasted like, he would say it was somewhere between intoxication and euphoria. This high didn't just bring F.E; it brought "understanding."

Having just crossed the life-and-death boundary, Benny's human soul seemed to have shed a layer of arrogance, truly beginning to align with the plant’s instincts. Closing his eyes, he manually disconnected the optical vision and artificial sensors provided by his Tier F clearance. He didn't want to look at everything through the lens of a system screen anymore. He wanted to truly "see" this world in the form of a tree.

When the holographic UI vanished, darkness descended, but it wasn't blindness.

A sensory network, primitive and incredibly subtle, gradually unfolded within his consciousness. He felt the temperature. He "saw" the variations in underground moisture. A fine root hair on the left was plunging into a pocket of decaying organic humus rich in minerals, while two auxiliary roots on the right hung suspended, reaching out to catch tiny dew droplets in the air.

Above his head, clusters of light photons tapped gently on the epidermis of his leaf. Instinctively, Benny focused his will. The leaf slowly twisted with a sluggish speed, angling itself perfectly to catch the sun, just like a sunflower turning toward the sun.

Sinking deeper into this state of absolute stillness, Benny began to notice a microscopic shift. He realized he... was growing.

Automatically, persistently, and incredibly slowly. If measured by human concepts of time, it was only nudging forward about 1 millimeter every half hour. Benny reopened his "eyes" (optical sensors), staring intently at the nearest young tendril.

"If I can guide my leaves to catch the sun... could I guide the way it grows?"

A flicker of hope sparked in his eyes. Despite almost dying from that stupid "overclocking" stunt earlier, the prospect of being able to move was truly alluring. This time, he was more cautious. The fact that a plant body possessed so few senses suddenly became an absolute advantage. There were no sounds or tactile sensations to distract him from sensing the metabolic flow within himself.

Benny’s entire body vibrated slightly even without the intervention of the wind.

Before his own astonished eyes, the lime-green tendrils quivered, stretching forward like a small snake, zigzagging and winding toward the same direction. The process stopped abruptly when Benny hurriedly cut off the energy flow. He gasped (in his mind).

The tendrils had grown exactly 2 centimeters longer. Looking back at the status panel, he calculated with bitterness: 0.0016 F.E had evaporated in just about 3 to 4 short seconds. It had nearly wiped out 15% of the total energy reserves he had gathered over more than 200 days.

"Too expensive," he muttered, feeling the fatigue from deep within his core.

However, an invisible smirk curled. He had found a way to move. Though costly and useless for combat, it was his golden ticket out of being an immobile log.

Benny intended to close his eyes and adjust his leaf angle to continue "grinding" back the wasted F.E when suddenly, the area of sunlight shining on his body decreased significantly. A shadow fell, obscuring his largest and most efficient photosynthetic leaf.

"A cloud?" Benny muttered in his mind.

But it wasn’t. Along with the shade came a physical weight crushing down on the petiole, causing it to sag, and a microscopic vibration transmitted through the phloem: *Skritch... scratch...*

He hurriedly refocused his optical sensors. When the image reached sharp resolution, the "blood" (sap) in his veins ran cold. Perched arrogantly on his precious leaf was no cloud shadow. It was a grasshopper.

*Crunch! Crunch!*

The serrated mandibles worked coldly. There was no sensation of pain like a knife cutting human flesh, but Benny felt a void—a terrifying emptiness carving deep into his very soul. With just two clean bites echoing with a pleasant crunching sound (at least for the grasshopper), one-third of Benny's largest leaf vanished into that screaming stomach. Blocks of his energy reserves disappeared cell by cell into the mouth of the herbivorous monster.

Its appearance wasn't overtly monstrous—no crazed red eyes or spiked armor emitting a dark aura. It was simply a light brown grasshopper, half the size of an adult human's palm. Its compound eyes observed the vine beneath its feet with apathetic detachment.

But as soon as Benny tossed out a scan command, the system returned unsettling parameters:

[Biological scan...] 

[Invasive species detected. Arthropod structure and respiratory system indicate native habitat: Grassland/Steppe.]

 [Physiological state: Debilitated due to environmental maladaptation. Incipient localized Chaos corruption detected.]

 [Warning: Natural predator detected.]

Benny instantly understood his tragedy. In this waterlogged primeval forest, the leaves of surrounding trees were half a centimeter thick, scruffy and tough as cowhide. The digestive system of a stray steppe grasshopper used to chewing soft grass was on strike. It was a picky eater, and it was starving.

The appearance of Benny, a tender, succulent young vine with a paper-thin epidermis, was nothing short of a gourmet salad served on a silver platter for someone on a hunger strike.

Hope seemed to have realized the gravity of the situation. Her holographic image paced back and forth in the air before abruptly stopping.

"Think of a way to stall, you idiot!" With that single sentence, her image vanished, diving into The Organization's data archive.

"Hey, hey, don't leave me like this!" Benny screamed in silence.

With no response, he could only focus on finding a way to save himself. His thoughts calculated at light speed, and the result was a desperate dead end. He had just spent 0.0016 F.E. to extend a few tendrils by a mere 2 centimeters. Even if he poured every drop of his remaining energy into a single tendril, it would only grow 10 to 20 cm at most, not nearly enough to save him.

He grew even more horrified as he saw those sickle-like mouthparts moving down toward his main stem. Panic left no room for logic. His survival instinct flared, and he burned through nearly his entire energy reserve in a few frantic seconds.

Cells worked so hard that sap actually oozed from the stem. Two tendrils from the nearest leaf axil snapped out like springs. But before the fiber could even fully loop around the monster's neck, the grasshopper startled and vaulted away, landing several meters off. It looked around silently before quickly crawling back toward Benny to finish its interrupted meal.

Just as despair was about to swallow Benny whole, Hope’s hologram flickered and reappeared radiantly.

"Benny, Benny, I found a way to save you!" she shouted excitedly, waving the data files she had just scavenged.
