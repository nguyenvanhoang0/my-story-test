Darkness. Dampness. And a suffocating confinement.

Those were the first signals Benny's brain registered. No sensation of limbs. No heartbeat. His consciousness felt compressed into a narrow straw, encased by a cold, fibrous shell.

"Wake up, wake up Mr. 404, you've been unconscious for 212 days. Get up already!"

A voice echoed in his head, sounding both familiar and strange. Immediately after, his entire body shuddered as if injected with adrenaline.

Benny "opened his eyes." In reality, he opened the stomata and photoreceptors on his leaf surfaces.

The world rushed into his consciousness in crisp 4K resolution, but the perspective was incredibly bizarre. He was looking from the bottom up. Towering canopies obscured the light, and tree roots as massive as pythons slithered across the damp, mossy ground.

Benny tried to bring a hand up to rub his eyes. *Swish.* A green tendril whipped through the air.

He wanted to scream: "What the hell is this?" But lacking vocal cords, he only emitted the rustling sound of leaves brushing against each other.

[Welcome, Employee 404, to Primeval World 0.6.2.8.A.]

The mechanical voice of the Virtual Assistant rang out in his mind, pulling him from his daze.

"Report body status," Benny ordered out of professional reflex, even though internally he was screaming because he couldn't feel his mouth anywhere.

[Acknowledged.] Entry Identity: Parasitic Vine (Epipremnum aureum - Primeval Variant). Status: Severe malnutrition. Nutrition/Energy Saturation Rate: \~2.2%. Sync Rate: 34.01%. [Warning]: This world currently registers you as an Outlander.

The assistant's monotonous voice echoed in his head.

A sensation akin to a "sigh" washed over his consciousness. The newly awakened young leaves drooped, exhausted. It was as if his weary soul was weighing down this fragile vine.

He tried to rummage through his memories. He remembered working for The Organization. He remembered throwing something down here. But what was the organization's name? Who was his boss? Why had he turned into a plant? It was all a thick fog.

[Attention]: Memories sealed under Level C Security Protocol. To prevent information leakage about the organization during the mission, your mind must be wiped as clean as a blank slate.

"Mission? What mission?" Ah right, he suddenly realized.

"Assistant, check the mission."

Instantly, the assistant opened the mission panel in his mind.

[Mission]: Restore Providence and retrieve items affecting the world's development. World Code: 0.6.2.8.A. 

Current State: Primeval Era. 

Predetermined Future: Become the foundation of an Apex Magical Civilization. Variable 

Emerged: Planetary Providence severely depleted. Revised Future: Destroyed by a meteorite, the world's progress set back by 2.3 million years. 

Calculated time until impact: 33 days and 23 hours. 

Main Mission: Restore the world's Providence. 

Sub-Mission: Retrieve item ???. 

Access Level: Tier F Information Retrieval Rights. 

Allocated Budget: 12,000 W\.E.C. 

Coordinator's Note]: Please leave no personal mark or trace of your existence prior to Branch Merging.

"A meteor? Seriously? This is only my first mission."

Staring at the "33 days" figure, then down at his vine body that wasn't even two meters long, he sighed—a frustrated reverse-photosynthesis.

After a few minutes of silence to accept this grim reality, Benny noticed something off. The Virtual Assistant's cadence break, a very faint exhale in the low-frequency band that a standard AI system would never possess.

He smirked in his imagination, trying to act professional as he stared into an empty void.

"I know you're there, Hope," Benny transmitted the thought, his tone flat.

The mechanical voice died out. In its place was an annoyed "Tsk."

A point of light flashed in the darkness, quickly expanding into the projection of a girl. Her compression ratio was down to one-tenth, making her look like a miniature model hovering in mid-air.

Her long white dress swayed gently with the holographic static. Her white hair was tied high, neat, and full of life. Her bare feet stood on thin air, as firmly planted as if she were stepping on solid ground.

But her eyes... Deep dark circles were etched beneath them, as if she hadn't slept in days.

"Tsk. Couldn't you at least fake being surprised? It takes the other guys days to figure it out." Hope was irritated that her little prank had been ruined.

A leaf rustled, as if shrugging. Benny stared at Hope's tiny projection, a murky glint flashing in his "eyes."

"Hey Hope, do you see that flying lizard perched on the branch over there?" Benny asked, directing her attention to a creature the size of a helicopter stalking its prey not far away.

"I see it. It's just a low-tier mutated Drake variant." She narrowed her eyes and smirked, "Hey, don't tell me you're getting cold feet already?"

Benny stared at the hovering projection, the words he meant to say caught in his throat, but he shamelessly grumbled anyway:

"One sneeze and I'm deleted. I don't have hands to hold a gun, I don't have legs to run, and my total energy output isn't even enough to light up an LED bulb for five minutes. What exactly do you want me to use to stop a meteor? The power of friendship?"

"According to The Organization's calculations..."

"Calculations my ass," Benny interrupted, his mindset becoming lazy and pragmatic. "Log this action report for me. Employee 404 declares mission flagged as FUBAR. Switching to passive survival mode."

His mind was made up. This mission was a trap, or some cruel joke by the higher-ups. He wasn't going to throw himself into an early grave. He would just cling to the base of this tree, absorb light, drink drops of dew, and wait until the mission timer ran out or the meteor fell on his head.

"Mute notifications. Don't bother me unless I'm getting eaten by a caterpillar."

Benny curled his leaves, trying to blend into the environment. He decided to apply nature's most basic and pragmatic survival strategy: Lie flat and play dead.

Hope and the assistant: "....."

"Request denied!" She cleared her throat, trying to sound serious. "You threw the mission item down here, the protocol is locked in. Read the mission panel carefully, you idiot, are you trying to make me write a rep—"

The noisy babbling of his little friend faded out, blending into the chirping of insects. The Volume slider on the Control Panel had been dragged all the way down to 1. Perfect! The audio pollutant was gone.

But the angry white silhouette was still blocking most of his vision. He tried to stretch his "eyes" to peek through the narrow gaps, struggling to observe the scenery behind her.

This forest wasn't just growing, it was exploding—a biological population explosion, as if a bio-bomb had been dropped on it. The flora here grew out of control; from the giant trees down to the vines, parasitic plants, and undergrowth.

It was as if being even slightly smaller than their neighbors meant a death sentence.

Except for him, a puny vine about two meters long with just a few small leaves. And realistically, with this physique, he could only catch tiny slivers of light slipping through their gaps.

If he had an olfactory sense, he bet this place would be reeking of damp, moldy moss, mixed with a cloyingly sweet nectar scent that would make one sick to their stomach...

Wait, nectar?

Benny shifted his optical sensors to the left. Turns out he had a neighbor. It was a brightly colored vine dotted with flowers, swaying flirtatiously to invite the bees and butterflies.

Looking down at his thin Pothos frame, Benny cursed enviously under his breath: 'Just where the hell did this handsome bastard come from?'

By instinct, he invoked his authority to inspect it.

[Biological scan...] [New biological variant detected (baseline organism). DNA match rate with "Parasitic Vine (*Vividiflora mellifera*)" of the Source World: 98.9%.] [You are authorized to designate this species in the Central Database.]

Benny's eyes, or whatever served as their replacement, lit up with a glint of amusement. He issued a command to inspect himself once more.

[Biological scan...] [New biological variant detected (baseline organism). DNA match rate with "Parasitic Vine (*Epipremnum aureum*)" of the Source World: 99.1%.] [You are authorized to designate this species in the Central Database.]

Oh, to think he could actually name himself. Just look at this '404' designation; that was no name for a human being. Waving a few leaves, he gleefully began designing a badass name for himself, the kind that reeked of ultimate authority just by hearing it.

[Name: Reality-Devouring Demon Vine already exists, please choose another name.]

"What the hell! Which bastard beat me to it?"

Narrowing his "eyes" to ignore a few pop-up messages, Benny designed yet another name for himself. ...

[Name confirmed: The Crownless Emperor of Infinite Entangled Fate.] [+1 Contribution Point awarded.]

Table slam (with a leaf). Honestly, if it weren't for the reward notification, he would have scolded those name-stealing bastards to hell and back.

Even though contribution points only had any real effect when counted in the millions, he had already committed to slacking off. At least when the higher-ups checked the logs, they'd see Employee 404 was engaged in diligent "field reconnaissance" instead of just sleeping through the apocalypse.

[Notice: Central Server intervention detected in Support System!]

*Crash... Cough... Cough...* The chaotic sounds of things breaking and violent coughing rang out. In a split second, the volume redlined, blasting into Benny's mind like a detonating bomb.

"Cough... cough... You idiot, what did you just do?! If the guys in the Research Department find out, they'll tear your house down! I have zero interest in dragging your dissected corpse out of their lab."

He was only slightly startled, but not scared in the least. On the contrary, he smugly rustled his leaves. His eyes were glued to her white dress, now stained with pink splotches. Well, she was drinking that sugar-coma-inducing soda again.

"Hey, have you ever seen a plant as smart as me? Just the mere fact that I'm here makes me worthy of that title."

Amidst his smugness, Benny suddenly froze. "Wait a minute, how did you turn your audio back on? You don't have the authorization!"

Hope fumbled for a beat, her eyes darting away. Her lips pursed slightly before relaxing as she quickly regained her confidence.

"It's Overseer privileges, you idiot!"

"Overseer?" Benny's leaves furrowed slightly. "Aren't you Support Staff?"

"I'm both!" She tilted her chin up so high he could practically see up her nostrils.

Hmm, something was off. There was no mistaking that reaction; she was definitely hiding a massive secret.

Benny tilted his canopy slightly to one side, silently staring at her with curious eyes. The intense, scrutinizing silence from the plant was so palpable that Hope couldn't stand it for more than a minute.

She spun around, taking large strides through the air toward his neighbor. Her tone was stiff, clearly deflecting: 

"Alright, focus on the mission. You'd better adjust your current state. Look at that perfect specimen next door. If you weren't occupying this vine, I would have picked that one. Try eating some more dirt."

"No." 

"What?" 

"I said no." 

"Care to repeat that?"

Benny paused for a beat, carefully evaluating her movements. Hmm, she isn't going to detonate just yet!

"No."

Hope whipped around, glaring at the vine lounging limply on its roots. Dammit! This broke bastard was slacking off again, and he still owed her a birthday party.

"Come on..." Hope softened her voice. She pointed at the radiantly blooming vine in the background, trying to force the most pathetic "puppy-dog" face possible, while simultaneously suppressing the urge to uproot this stubborn weed in front of her and stir-fry it.

"Just look at this world. It's so beautiful and full of life, how could you have the heart to just abandon it?"

Benny looked from her to the neighboring plant, that handsome bastard... 

"No." 

Why should he sacrifice himself to save someone better-looking than him? Well... wait, is it even a guy?

Hope: "..." 

This is bad. This guy's brain structure has definitely turned to mush. Why the hell did I voluntarily take on this damn project in the first place?!

Stomping her foot hard on the empty air, Hope's holographic projection began to shatter into thousands of tiny light particles, slowly fading away. "Hmph! I'm not going to bother with a stubborn thing like you anymore!" She grumbled her final sentence before the connection signal vanished entirely.

Benny’s tendrils waved mockingly in the air, seeing the little tyrant off. But as the projection flickered out, the tedious silence of the primeval forest rushed back in.

Benny sighed. He couldn’t walk, couldn’t move, couldn’t even reach out to scratch an itch on his bark. The ultimate helplessness of a creature "imprisoned" in place made his brain itch with madness. There was nothing to do but stare at everything around him with the spiteful glare of a shut-in.

He shifted his attention to the neighbor—the one swaying its colorful flowers to invite bees and butterflies, a hundred times more radiant and beautiful than his own scrawny pothos body. Jealousy flared up. He scanned it, pouring all his spite into the designation key.

[Name confirmed: Frivolous Flowerpot Awaiting Decapitation (*Vividiflora mellifera*).] [+1 Contribution Point.]

"Even mosquito legs are meat," Benny thought, narrowing his eyes at the round number "2" in his contribution point pool.

Driven by a resource scavenging mentality and the bitter resentment of someone who could do nothing but judge, he began sweeping his optical sensors across his surroundings. Anything within his line of sight was dragged into a scan and slapped with quirky labels born of a personal vendetta.

An ant crawling across a root? [Name confirmed: Blindly Fanatical Porter 01.]

A patch of moss emitting a faint fluorescent glow? [Name confirmed: Neglected Fluorescent Doormat.]

Even a glob of sticky, pungent sap falling from the giant canopy and landing on his leaf was designated as: [Obnoxious Territorial Spittle].

He didn't know how much time had passed; he only knew that the sunlight was now hitting him at an angle of approximately 90 degrees, flickering weakly through the overhead foliage.

His total contribution points had reached 122, but his interest died as quickly as it had appeared. This game was getting boring once he realized the same few organisms just kept repeating themselves, and contribution points wouldn't help him survive past tomorrow.

He reopened the status panel to check his photosynthetic yield after several hours.

2.2%

Benny blue-screened. All the energy he had synthesized so far was only enough to nudge the figure from "approximately 2.2" to a "flat 2.2."

As it turned out, because this body was so pathetic and suffered from constant energy leakage into the environment, the "starvation-level interest rate" after a 212-day coma had only allowed his savings to crawl up by a measly 0.0124 F.E (2.2%).

This ragtag pile of assets sat right on the edge of "The Dead Zone." There wasn't enough Discretionary Energy to accumulate growth for even a single new bud, let alone any kind of mutation or defense.

How was he supposed to "play dead" like this? If he just lay flat for 33 days, he’d still be a two-meter vine waiting to be flattened by a falling meteor.

His resource optimization mindset began to surge, overriding his complacency.

Photosynthesis and water absorption were essentially just chemical reactions and capillary physics. The current turtle-pace was only because the plant was running in its "default idle mode." *I have a human consciousness,* he thought. *I can overclock its output.*

The moment the thought formed, Benny focused his entire mind on his physical structure. He envisioned himself as a high-capacity pump. He commanded the stomata on his leaf surfaces to open to their absolute limit to intake CO2. Simultaneously, he forced the roots beneath the soil to contract harder to pull water up, switching his entire respiratory system from natural mode to "manual breathing."

In the very next second, he realized exactly why Mother Nature never allowed plants to possess human intelligence.
